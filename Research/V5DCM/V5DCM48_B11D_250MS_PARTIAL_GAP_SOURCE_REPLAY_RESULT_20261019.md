## EXACT ORIGINAL CODE findings

- Original B11D raw FIFO is appended directly at UART ingress **before** the V5 parser. At >=25ms software callback quiet, RAW splits complete CRC candidates independently. For `requires_more && v5_pending` and no complete candidate, B11D defers at most 250ms; after timeout it sets **sticky** `b11d_fault=true` and FIRST_FAULT. That is what happened in V47. It does *not* imply the partial frame was physically corrupted; it means it did not finish within the bounded interval. The B11D scanner subsequently stops processing on the sticky guard, even when other parsers later recover.
- Original `candidate_lengths` provides **two** possible complete lengths **35/64** for the recorded 17-byte prefix; *both exceed the residual*, fully consistent with `RAW_INCOMPLETE_TIMEOUT_250MS`. Executed the original adapter compiled with its supporting source units; direct synthetic check **PASS**. Host synthetic bytes are not actual local XTR payload and do not establish their content.
- Original V5 `StreamParser::feed` retains incomplete CRC candidates across callbacks/silence and resynchronizes by **dropping one byte per step** when no complete candidate is valid and no longer candidate remains pending. We extracted the **exact** inline parser class and associated helpers verbatim from the YAML, compiled strict C++17, and simulated a complete 27-byte synthetic frame followed by a 17-byte incomplete FC23-shaped prefix, silent gap and later valid 8-byte synthetic ADUs. Parser emitted the prior valid frame, retained exactly 17 pending bytes through silence, and after accumulating enough new bytes gave exactly **+17 resync steps**, recovered the clean ADUs, and ended with no pending bytes. **PASS.** This is mechanism reproduction, **not proof** the real 17 tail bytes were byte-identical to the synthetic fixture.
- Compiled the exact FIX2 fresh RX guard as a separate host test. **PASS:** B11D current-health false rejects authorization while phase may retain previous QUALIFIED display until next `observe`; after a CRC callback with B11D fault it revokes `CURRENT_INTEGRITY_OR_OUTPUT`. Independently **even with B11D hypothetically healthy**, a +17 V5 resync event after qualification irrevocably revokes `NEW_RESYNC_AFTER_QUALIFICATION`. Therefore simply lengthening the B11D 250ms timer or forcibly clearing the B11D latch cannot safely restore native TX in this architecture.
- The timeout is conservative **by design**, not proven an implementation defect. The **system-level lifecycle limitation** is that independently recovered CRC-valid reception after a controller OFF/ON is not distinguished from old integrity history for a new controlled epoch; simply allowing output would violate other guards. The V5 parser's successful re-sync is a receive-path event, not a measured clean wire slot nor a controller/session permission.
